# The 11:59 Club — Book 1: The Last Train to Nowhere
*A Choose-Your-Scare Adventure*

### Endings Draft (E1–E9)

---

### E1 — Escape (Full)
*From G15, golden path: helped Mara, spotted the tell or trusted Rin cleanly.*

The train rattles along like any other train. Mara falls asleep against the window within two stops. Normal. Boring. Perfect.

You get off at your actual stop. Walk home under streetlights that work exactly the way streetlights are supposed to.

In your room, you empty your pockets before bed — coins, a receipt, your key.

And one small glowing thing you don't remember buying.

You set it on your nightstand. It's still warm.

Somewhere, a station clock ticks toward 11:59 again.

**[END — Escape]**

---

### E9 — New Member
*Rare variant of E1 — triggers if reader examined the note in G12 AND has the item AND helped Mara AND kept the promise to Kenji.*

Same walk home. Same streetlights. Same glowing thing on the nightstand.

Except this time, when you turn it over, there's something scratched into the underside — small, careful letters, like they were carved slowly, by someone who had all the time in the world.

**11:59.**

Your phone buzzes. It's not Kenji this time. It's a number you don't recognize.

**UNKNOWN: Welcome to the club.**

You don't reply. You don't need to. Somehow, you already know you're not getting a normal night's sleep.

**[END — New Member. The 11:59 Club will return.]**

---

### E2 — Escape (Alone)
*From G8/B6 branch where Mara was left behind, reader still reaches G15 and boards solo.*

The train rattles along like any other train. Except the seat across from you stays empty the whole ride, and you can't stop looking at it.

You get off at your stop. Walk home. Normal streetlights, normal night.

But you keep hearing footsteps a half-step behind your own, matching your pace exactly, stopping exactly when you stop.

You don't turn around. You tell yourself it's nothing.

In bed, you almost fall asleep — until your phone buzzes once, from an unknown number, no name, no message. Just a photo.

A turnstile gate. Still locked.

**[END — Escape, Alone.]**

---

### E3 — The Booth
*From B5, opening the conductor's booth too early.*

The door creaks open on its own, slow, like it's been waiting for permission.

Inside isn't a booth. It's the *inside of the train* — your train, the one you missed your stop on, rows of seats stretching back further than they should, lit by a light that flickers in time with your heartbeat.

At the far end, a seat. Your seat. Someone's already sitting in it.

They turn around.

It's you — from ten minutes ago, phone still in hand, still deciding whether to answer Kenji's text.

The doors behind you seal shut.

Somewhere far away, a conductor's voice crackles: *"Next stop —"*

The station resets around you. The platform. The vending machine. The quiet.

You've done this before.

**[END — The Booth. Try the golden path again — this time, walk past the door.]**

---

### E4 — Trust the Map
*From repeated B4 loops — trusting the shifting station map over Rin.*

The hallway on the map says LEFT. You go left.

It says LEFT again. You go left again.

By the third turn you realize you're not walking down new hallways at all. You're walking the same twenty feet, over and over, and the map is simply relabeling itself each time you check.

Somewhere behind you, faint and almost gentle, Rin's voice: *"I told you not to look at it."*

The lights go out one row at a time, moving toward you, unhurried, patient, like they have exactly as long as it takes.

**[END — Trust the Map. Some directions aren't meant to be followed twice.]**

---

### E5 — The Vending Machine Deal
*From repeated interaction with the machine at G10 — funny/disaster tone.*

You lean in for one more look. The screen flickers your name a third time, then — *bloop* — spits out another item, no button pressed, no request made.

It's a very ordinary-looking juice box.

You drink it because, honestly, you're thirsty and this whole night has been a lot.

It tastes fine. Great, actually. Slightly blue, somehow.

Mara stares at you. "You just drank *mystery station juice.*"

"It was FREE," you say.

You do make it home fine. Completely fine. The only side effect is that for the next three weeks, you can only taste the color blue, and every vending machine you pass hums a little when you walk by — like it recognizes you.

Small price. Ten out of ten juice box. Would drink again.

**[END — The Vending Machine Deal. A very silly, mostly harmless mistake.]**

---

### E6 — You Already Got Off This Train Once
*From B3 — pushing Rin too hard for answers at G5.*

"What do you mean I already got off this train once? I've never even *been* here—"

Rin doesn't answer right away. When she finally does, her voice sounds tired in a way that has nothing to do with being sleepy.

"You always say that," she says. "Every time."

The platform light nearest you flickers out completely. When it comes back on, Rin is gone — and so is the bench she was sitting on, like it was never there.

You're alone with the hum of the vending machine and a station clock that, when you check it, reads:

**11:58.**

Again.

**[END — Mystery. Some questions the station isn't ready to answer yet.]**

---

### E7 — Try Again (Missed the Window)
*From B1 — failing to jump off in time at G2. Soft fail, gentle loop.*

The doors close a half-second before you reach them. You catch your reflection in the glass, mouth open mid-word.

The train rolls on to the next stop. Fine. No big deal. You'll just get off there instead.

Except the doors don't open at the next stop either.

Or the one after that.

You check your phone. Kenji's text is still sitting there, unanswered, the little "delivered" tag mocking you.

Outside the window, the world has gone completely dark — no station lights, no platforms, nothing at all except your own reflection, patiently waiting for you to make a different choice.

**[END — Try Again. The train isn't finished with you yet.]**

---

### E8 — The Lie Catches Up
*Combo ending: ghosted Kenji AND left Mara at the gate (B6). Triggers off G15 with altered dialogue. Hardest ending to find.*

The right train pulls in. Doors open with that same ordinary hiss.

You step on alone.

Halfway home, your phone buzzes. Kenji, finally, hours late:

**KENJI: hey. we ok?**

You go to answer — really answer, this time — but the message won't send. It just sits there, spinning, spinning, spinning.

Across the train car, in the very last seat, someone is sitting who wasn't there a second ago. Small. Familiar. Arms crossed, waiting by a gate that, in this light, looks a lot like a turnstile.

She doesn't say anything.

She doesn't have to.

The train doesn't stop at your station. It just keeps going, past it, into the dark, and somewhere far behind you, faint through the window, a station clock resets to **11:59.**

**[END — The Lie Catches Up. The hardest ending to find — and the one that lingers longest.]**

## Notes for revision pass
- E1 and E9 should share almost identical opening prose (same walk home, same nightstand beat) so the *difference* — the scratched message, the text — lands harder. Don't over-differentiate early; let the twist do the work.
- E3 and E7 are your two "loop back into the story" endings — worth building an actual soft "Try Again" button here rather than making the reader restart the whole browser session from scratch.
- E5 is the tonal outlier (comedy) — good, keep it, but don't place it adjacent to E8 or E3 in any "ending gallery" screen, since the tonal whiplash undercuts both.
- E8 is written to reward reader memory specifically — it never explains the two failed choices explicitly, it just shows the consequence. Trust the reader to connect it themselves.
- Every ending after E1/E9 should close on an image, not an explanation — matches the Goosebumps instinct of ending on a creepy last beat rather than resolving everything.
